**As of:** 2026-10-21  
**Registry IDs:** `AF-001` onward. These IDs create a new, non-colliding registry; the preceding text is preserved verbatim, including its local numbering.  
**Status at registration:** All new rows are `OPEN`. These are proposed operational hypotheses and forecasts, not claims that their outcomes are already known.

## Operating constitution

1. **Freeze the test before the result.** The outcome variable, denominator, deadline, comparison group, threshold, and authoritative evidence source are written down at registration. The thresholds below are proposed decision thresholds, not universal constants.
2. **Append, do not rewrite.** Never delete or silently edit a row. A corrected definition or changed forecast receives a new ID and names the row it supersedes. The old row remains visible.
3. **Separate claim, observation, and decision.** The claim ledger stores what was expected; the evidence ledger stores observations and provenance; the allocation ledger records what changed because of the result.
4. **Do not confuse survival with proof.** `SURVIVED` means the specified test did not falsify the claim; it does not establish universal truth. Use `FALSIFIED` when the registered threshold fails, `INCONCLUSIVE` when the test cannot decide, and `EXPIRED—UNRESOLVED` when its deadline passes without adequate evidence.
5. **Count the whole cost.** Where a claim concerns efficiency, include inference, data access, human review, coordination, rework, failures, maintenance, and externalities—not only the most visible metric.
6. **Preserve the denominator.** “Every child,” “broad deployment,” “successful treatment,” “productivity,” and “peace” are not measurable until eligibility, scale, quality, and time windows are specified.
7. **Allow the framework to lose.** Ukubona's own five-rung architecture is treated as a model to compare against simpler alternatives, not as a privileged description exempt from testing.

## Ledger · New entries

### AF-001 · Epistemic routing versus fluent completion

- **Domain / stage:** AI cognition; II–IV (`R_ij` → `A_ijk / Γ_ijk` → collisions with the live milieu).
- **Registered claim:** On evidence-dependent questions, a system that decides when to search, retrieve, calculate, ask for missing inputs, or abstain will outperform the same base model answering from internal completion alone.
- **Test and deadline:** By **2027-12-31**, compare both systems on at least 500 independently authored tasks across three domains. Score verifiable correctness and source support; log latency and full marginal cost.
- **Falsifier:** The routed system fails to improve verified correctness by at least **8 percentage points**, or its cost-adjusted utility is no better than the completion-only baseline.
- **Evidence to retain:** Frozen task set, scoring rubric, tool logs, citations, latency/cost logs, and adjudication disagreements.

### AF-002 · Context-sensitive resource allocation versus one universal mode

- **Domain / stage:** Learning architecture; I–V, with emphasis on V (`Budget → payoff / survival / graveyard / unknowns`).
- **Registered claim:** A router that sends routine, stable tasks to reflex/cache; unfamiliar tasks to specialist models; and volatile, high-stakes, or contradictory tasks to deeper evidence search will beat both “always search” and “always answer immediately.”
- **Test and deadline:** By **2027-12-31**, test at least 1,000 tasks divided in advance among routine, distribution-shifted, and time-sensitive categories. Compare verified quality per total dollar and per minute against both fixed-policy baselines.
- **Falsifier:** The routed policy fails to deliver at least **10% higher cost-adjusted utility** than the better fixed-policy baseline on two separately constructed test sets.
- **Failure signature:** A router that merely adds complexity and latency, without improving the quality-cost frontier, has failed.

### AF-003 · Specialist-plus-generalist systems versus a single generalist

- **Domain / stage:** Model architecture; III–IV (`A_ijk / Γ_ijk` plus cross-domain collisions).
- **Registered claim:** Routing selected task classes to validated specialist systems, with a generalist orchestrating or resolving edge cases, will lower the cost of a verified correct result relative to a single generalist model.
- **Test and deadline:** By **2027-12-31**, test three predefined task classes, each with at least 300 held-out cases. Count routing mistakes and human corrections.
- **Falsifier:** The hybrid does not reduce cost per verified correct result by at least **20%**, or it loses more than **2 percentage points** of accuracy relative to the generalist.
- **Guardrail:** Do not count a specialist as successful because its narrow benchmark score is high if the complete routed workflow fails more often.

### AF-004 · AI-agent productivity after the rework bill

- **Domain / stage:** Enterprise economics; IV–V (collisions → allocation).
- **Registered claim:** In a defined production workflow, an AI agent will reduce the total cost per accepted deliverable, not merely increase drafts, actions, code commits, or raw output.
- **Test and deadline:** By **2027-12-31**, evaluate at least three workflows across at least two organizations, using a six-month pre/post or randomized comparison where feasible. Include model fees, integration, oversight, human review, rework, incidents, and maintenance.
- **Falsifier:** In at least two of the three workflows, fully loaded cost per accepted deliverable falls by **less than 20%**, or material-error rates worsen by more than **2 percentage points**.
- **Why this is not settled by output metrics:** A 2026 field-experiment paper reports a 26.08% increase in completed tasks among software developers using coding assistance across three experiments; that is evidence about those tasks, not proof of positive net returns for every agentic workflow. [Study](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00535).

### AF-005 · Public benchmark score versus fresh-task competence

- **Domain / stage:** AI measurement; II–III (representation and specialist evaluation).
- **Registered claim:** Scores on public static benchmarks materially overestimate competence on fresh, previously unreleased tasks from the same intended domain.
- **Test and deadline:** By **2027-06-30**, compare at least ten systems on public benchmarks and a matched, blinded set authored after the systems' evaluation targets are frozen. Match task type and difficulty; publish item-level scoring rules.
- **Falsifier:** The median score gap is **under 5 percentage points** across the matched suite. A gap of **10 points or more** supports the registered claim; the interval between those thresholds is recorded as inconclusive.
- **Mechanism caution:** A gap alone does not prove training-data contamination; difficulty mismatch, tool access, prompt sensitivity, and distribution shift must be separately tested. The contamination literature distinguishes static and dynamic evaluation and notes unresolved standardization challenges. [EMNLP 2025 survey](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.511/).

### AF-006 · Event streams versus static snapshots in personalized applications

- **Domain / stage:** Client apps and adaptive services; I–V (principal → representation → specialist → milieu collisions → allocation).
- **Registered claim:** When a user's circumstances change over time, an event-stream architecture will support timelier, more useful personalization than a system that relies chiefly on periodically refreshed static profiles.
- **Test and deadline:** By **2028-06-30**, compare the approaches in at least three live workflows. Predefine “actionable update,” measure median event-to-action latency and task completion, and track incorrect interventions, opt-outs, and privacy incidents.
- **Falsifier:** The event-stream design improves median event-to-action latency by **less than 30%** and does not improve task completion, or it creates a material increase in harmful or unwanted interventions.
- **Interpretation:** More telemetry is not automatically more intelligence. The stream must improve a consequential decision enough to justify its collection and governance costs.

### AF-007 · Data friction as an independent barrier to clinical science

- **Domain / stage:** Research infrastructure; II and V (permissions / representation → allocation of analyst time and resources).
- **Registered claim:** Reducing avoidable data-access and preparation friction increases the number and speed of valid research analyses, even when the scientific question and investigators are otherwise unchanged.
- **Test and deadline:** By **2028-10-21**, run a prospective, stepped-wedge, or carefully matched evaluation at multiple research sites. Measure time from approved question to analysis-ready dataset, plus the proportion of preregistered analyses completed within 12 months.
- **Falsifier:** A documented access-and-pipeline intervention reduces median data latency by **less than 25%** and does not raise completed preregistered analyses by at least **20%** across the participating sites.
- **Non-negotiable constraint:** Faster access counts only if consent, privacy, data provenance, and scientific validity remain intact.

### AF-008 · External calibration before clinical deployment

- **Domain / stage:** Clinical prediction; III–V (specialist model → real-world milieu → clinical allocation).
- **Registered claim:** A clinical prediction model that is externally validated and recalibrated for a new setting will be safer to use for probability-based decisions than the same frozen model evaluated only by its original development metrics.
- **Test and deadline:** By **2028-10-21**, test at least one prespecified model across five materially different sites. Report calibration, discrimination, subgroup performance, and decision consequences before and after recalibration.
- **Falsifier:** Recalibration fails to reduce absolute calibration error by at least **20% at four of five sites**, or achieves that reduction only by breaching a prespecified safety floor for sensitivity or subgroup performance.
- **Guardrail:** Better aggregate AUC alone does not count as confirmation.

### AF-009 · Retrieval-based AI tutoring versus explanation-only tutoring

- **Domain / stage:** Education and memory; III–IV (specialist tutor interacting with a learner over time).
- **Registered claim:** For a defined body of material, an AI tutor that requires spaced retrieval and feedback will produce better delayed retention than an otherwise comparable explanation-only tutor at equal study time.
- **Test and deadline:** By **2027-12-31**, run a preregistered randomized study with at least 300 learners, identical content and study-time limits, and a blinded retention assessment after 30 days.
- **Falsifier:** The retrieval arm's delayed-retention advantage is **less than 5 percentage points**, or its apparent gain disappears when study time and completion rates are accounted for.
- **Prior evidence, not proof of this specific design:** Meta-analytic and review literature supports spacing and retrieval practice in many learning settings; the incremental effect of a particular AI tutor remains testable. [Nature Reviews Psychology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-022-00089-1) · [2021 meta-analysis](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1310148).

### AF-010 · Adaptive budgeting versus prestige-based or fixed allocation

- **Domain / stage:** Enterprise and public-sector management; V (budget, payoff, survival, graveyard, unknowns).
- **Registered claim:** Allocating incremental budget by audited marginal outcomes—with explicit stop rules—will outperform allocation driven mainly by seniority, sunk costs, or narrative prestige.
- **Test and deadline:** By **2028-10-21**, compare at least 20 comparable projects, teams, or program units using a randomized or prespecified matched design. Record baseline resources, quality-adjusted outcomes, downstream costs, and failed projects retained or terminated.
- **Falsifier:** The outcome-led allocation policy improves quality-adjusted output per unit of resource by **less than 15%**, with no compensating improvement in risk or time-to-decision.
- **Counterfactual requirement:** Projects killed by the policy must be included; otherwise the ledger rewards only visible survivors and hides the graveyard.

### AF-011 · Attention generated by spectacle versus delivery of the underlying mission

- **Domain / stage:** Media, markets, and leadership; IV (collisions among milieu, principal, narrative, and technical artifact).
- **Registered claim:** For highly publicized corporate technology announcements, first-week attention is a poor proxy for delivery of the promised operational capability over the following 12–24 months.
- **Test and deadline:** Register a cohort of at least 50 announcements before their outcomes are known. Score attention from a fixed media sample, freeze each announcement's concrete promises, and independently verify delivery at 12 and 24 months.
- **Falsifier:** Attention intensity adds at least **0.10 out-of-sample R²** to prediction of verified delivery after accounting for resources, prior capability, and milestone maturity, across two held-out cohorts.
- **Scope note:** This tests the general “distraction” hypothesis; it does not assume that every spectacular announcement is empty or that publicity cannot be a rational investment.

### AF-012 · Renewables versus coal in global electricity generation

- **Domain / stage:** Energy transition; IV–V (environment and infrastructure collisions → generation mix and capital allocation).
- **Registered forecast:** Global electricity generation from renewables exceeds global coal-fired generation in **calendar year 2026**.
- **Deadline and falsifier:** Score using final, comparable global generation data published by **2027-12-31**. The forecast is falsified if renewables do **not** exceed coal under the same source's definitions and accounting boundary.
- **Basis:** This is a measurable external forecast, not an original Ukubona prediction: the IEA's 2026 Electricity Mid-Year Update projected that renewables would overtake coal in 2026. [IEA forecast](https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-mid-year-update-2026/executive-summary).

### AF-013 · Make “frozen conflict” a scoreable geopolitical forecast

- **Domain / stage:** Geopolitics; IV–V (milieu, coalitions, campaigns, bargaining, strategic outcome).
- **Registered claim:** Operationalizing Mearsheimer's “frozen conflict” argument is more informative than scoring a war as simply won or lost.
- **Operational definition to freeze before scoring:** A case qualifies only if a ceasefire or armistice holds for **12 consecutive months** while a major territorial or political dispute remains unresolved. Record separately whether fighting resumes, the settlement terms, territorial control, external support, and economic spillovers.
- **Scoring rule:** Score the US–Iran, Saudi–Houthi, and Russia–Ukraine cases separately against a declared date. Do not count a case as “frozen” merely because media attention fades or front lines move slowly.
- **Falsifier:** The thesis that **at least two of the three** cases meet that definition by **2028-12-31** is falsified if fewer than two qualify. If the record is insufficient to establish the ceasefire duration or unresolved-dispute condition, mark the case `INCONCLUSIVE` rather than choosing a convenient interpretation.
- **Scope note:** This row formalizes a test of the supplied video's argument; it does not independently certify every causal attribution or event description in the transcript.

### AF-014 · Is Ukubona's five-rung architecture better than a simpler model?

- **Domain / stage:** The framework itself; I–V.
- **Registered claim:** The five-rung structure—principal, representation, specialist, adaptive collisions, allocation—helps predict consequential failure modes and choose interventions better than a simpler one-stage or three-stage account.
- **Test and deadline:** By **2028-10-21**, preregister at least 100 cases from two or more domains. Have independent assessors classify cases with the five-rung framework and at least one simpler baseline, without seeing outcomes; compare predictive calibration, intervention quality, and application cost.
- **Falsifier:** The five-rung model fails to improve held-out prediction or decision quality by **10%**, or any gains are erased by its extra coding and coordination cost.
- **Consequence:** If falsified, revise the architecture in a new row—or discard it. Do not redefine a rung after seeing the cases.

### AF-015 · Does a forecast ledger improve calibration?

- **Domain / stage:** Epistemology and organizational learning; I–V, with V closing the loop.
- **Registered claim:** Requiring forecasts to have explicit probabilities, deadlines, outcomes, and postmortems will improve forecast calibration compared with narrative-only discussion.
- **Test and deadline:** By **2028-10-21**, collect at least 200 resolvable predictions from comparable individuals or teams. Where feasible, assign one group a structured ledger and the other usual practice; score probabilities with a proper scoring rule such as the Brier score.
- **Falsifier:** The ledger group does not achieve at least **15% lower mean Brier score** than the comparison group, or the apparent improvement disappears after controlling for forecast difficulty and question selection.
- **Anti-gaming rule:** Count missed, withdrawn, and inconvenient forecasts. Do not score only the predictions that turned out well.

### AF-016 · Does personalization earn the right to collect more data?

- **Domain / stage:** Privacy, product design, and public trust; I–V.
- **Registered claim:** More granular event collection is justified only when it creates measurable incremental user benefit over a less invasive design.
- **Test and deadline:** Before any high-granularity deployment, preregister a comparison of the proposed event-stream design against the least-data design that can plausibly deliver the service. Evaluate user benefit, error, opt-out, security incidents, and ongoing governance cost for at least 90 days.
- **Falsifier:** The higher-data design does not improve the primary user outcome by at least **10%**, or it materially worsens unwanted interventions, opt-outs, security exposure, or governance cost without a commensurate benefit.
- **Allocation implication:** “Unknowns” include privacy harms and future misuse, not just missing product revenue.

**The closing question for every row is not merely “Were we right?” It is “What will we do differently if we are wrong?”** If the answer is “nothing,” the entry is commentary, not yet an operational learning loop.
